# Choose the verifier's model family by measurement

Status: Proposed. Becomes Accepted when the owner records the measured rates and the resulting choice in issue #18.

## Problem

The answer verifier runs on the same model as the answering agent (`gpt-5.6-terra`, at low reasoning effort), and it also writes the corrected text that pass 2 re-verifies and releases. Two separate causes could make such a verifier miss a wrong answer: a model is less likely to catch its own family's errors, and low effort gives it less room to look. Nobody has measured either. The seeded-fault benchmark ([verifier-benchmark](../operations/verifier-benchmark.md)) exists for this and has not yet run live.

## Decision

Decide the verifier's model family, and its effort, from the benchmark and from nothing else. The verifier's bounds from issue #18 (30-second deadline, two passes, no retries) are fixed inputs to the experiment; a candidate that needs more is disqualified, not accommodated.

Three arms, seeded and originals fixtures only: the current model at low effort as the baseline, the current model at medium effort, and `claude-sonnet-5-5` at low effort. Effort is held equal across families so that family is the variable being tested. A candidate takes both roles, judge and author of the correction, because the release gate verifies the exact corrected text regardless of who wrote it.

A candidate wins only when all three hold: no seeded error kind is caught less often and at least one is caught more often; clean originals are withheld no more often; and runs that end unavailable at the deadline stay under 2 percent. More catches bought with more withholding is a loss, because release validation caps withheld answers at a count tied to the benchmark size (4 of 47 at the time of writing, in `corpus-release-policy.json`).

Two rules keep the comparison honest. First, a difference counts only when it clears noise: the benchmark's verdicts vary between calls and its three repeats of one item are not independent, so a kind rises only when the candidate's rate is above the baseline's Wilson 95 percent upper bound, drops only when it is below the baseline's lower bound, and is otherwise unchanged; the same reading applies to the originals' withhold rate; and the per-item agreement columns must not worsen. Second, a catch by correction counts only when the corrected text matches the clean original outside the seeded span. A correction that removes the seed but changes something else was approved only by the candidate itself, which is the blind spot under test, so the benchmark reports those corrections separately and the win rule does not credit them.

Before any paid run: the two AI SDK provider packages are updated in range (issue #62), the verifier's effort becomes a `VERIFIER_REASONING_EFFORT` setting applied under both provider keys and recorded in the evaluation fingerprint, and the benchmark report prints latency and deadline aborts separately from other failures, reports corrections that stray from the clean original, and takes a hard spend stop separate from the start-up bound check. Spend is held to a $150 total that the owner enforces across arms: each arm is trialled at $5 first, its measured spend per run multiplied out to a forecast, and a full arm runs only when that forecast stays under $60. The script refuses to start when its printed worst-case bound (about $121 for the current model and $108 for the Claude arm on the two fixtures) exceeds the cap flag, and today that same flag is also its measured-spend stop, so the flag alone cannot hold an arm to $60. The benchmark therefore gains a separate hard spend stop, set to the arm's forecast ceiling, while the bound check keeps its flag at the printed bound. The refusal rule itself is unchanged. The owner runs every paid command.

**Cost swap (November 2026).** A cheaper model may replace the verifier without catching more, because the gate's safety depends on not catching less. The cheaper candidate is adopted when no seeded kind drops (the same Wilson reading as above), clean originals are withheld no more often, deadline aborts stay under 2 percent, and the per-item columns do not worsen. This is how `gpt-6.1-terra` (half the price of `gpt-5.6-terra`) is measured; the owner asked for the upgrade on 2026-11-01. It takes both roles, like any candidate, and is adopted in one commit with the qualification environment.

## Alternatives

Swap to a cross-family verifier on the strength of the argument alone. Rejected: the swap changes the evaluation fingerprint and so voids the release baseline; paying for a new baseline on an unmeasured hunch is the wrong order. Raise effort alone without a cross-family arm. Rejected as the only arm: if effort does not close the gap, the comparison would have to be run again. Judge with a different family but keep the agent's model authoring corrections. Rejected for now: two calls per correction and a code change, for a benefit the gate does not need.

## Consequences

The verifier model and effort are not changed in production until the measurement is in. A winning candidate is adopted at the next `release-baseline` regeneration, which issue #18 already requires before the next candidate run, so the swap costs no extra run. The verifier model is set in two places, the agent's code default and the pipeline qualification environment ([qualification preparation](../operations/qualification-preparation.md)), and the fingerprint reads the environment, so adoption changes both in the same commit or evaluation and production verify with different models. The judged-bad fixture and its 64 unconfirmed labels stay out of this decision. The summary lives under `docs/validation/verifier-benchmark-2026-10/`; raw results stay outside Git. Terms used here are defined in the root [glossary](../../CONTEXT.md).
